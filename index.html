— User Guide —

How it works

🗓
Set a date range
Choose any start and end date — from ancient history to the far future. The engine uses real Keplerian orbital mechanics to compute exact planet positions for every day.
🪐
Click any planet to track it
Click a planet in the 3D scene or in the list on the right — the camera locks onto it and a fact sheet slides in with mass, distance, day length, moons, temperature and more.
▶
Play, pause & speed up
Use Play/Pause to animate the simulation. The Speed slider lets you go from 0.1× to 50× — watch years pass in seconds or slow down to study a single orbit.
🔭
Navigate the 3D scene
Rotate with left-click drag, zoom with the scroll wheel, and pan with right-click drag. Switch to Top View for a clean overhead look at all orbital paths.
☰
Toggle layers
The ⚙ button lets you show or hide moons, asteroid belt, dwarf planets, Halley's Comet, space missions (Voyager 1 & 2, New Horizons) and live NASA near-Earth asteroid data.
🛸
Live NASA data
When enabled, real near-Earth asteroids fetched from the NASA NeoWs API are placed in the scene. Red dots mark potentially hazardous objects.
